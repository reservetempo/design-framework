braun and the ulm school, 1955–1990s

Gute Form

Less, but better: a small CSS library of flat plates, push keys and printed lowercase labels, in the colours of the SK 4 radio-phonograph.

about

Gute Form (German for “good form”) is the sober, functional product culture of West Germany from the mid-1950s, centred on the Ulm School of Design (HfG Ulm, 1953–68) and Braun. From 1955 Hans Gugelot, Dieter Rams and their colleagues gave Braun radios, record players, shavers and calculators calm white, grey and black bodies, with colour kept for a control that matters. Rams summed it up as weniger, aber besser: less, but better.

This is the look as a small CSS library, gute-form.css: flat plates with a printed label strip, small lowercase labels, 1px seams, tight 2px corners and keys that sink when pressed. The colours are the SK 4 palette, after the 1956 radio-phonograph: warm white lacquer, a pale grey control plate, elm and anthracite. Press Tab to see the focus rings. Not affiliated with Braun.

Background: the visual research (history, traits, materials, type and six palettes), the component research and the palette comparison.

panels

A panel is one flat plate: a label strip with a small heading on the left and room for a key on the right, then the body.

Zones inside are split by a gap or a 1px rule, never by boxes in boxes. This one has the elm side strips of the SK 4 (gf-panel--trim).

Mittelwelle: medium wave, roughly 0.5 to 1.6 MHz. Further by night.

Langwelle: long wave, roughly 150 to 280 kHz. A few strong stations.

Lamps light in the second colour and always sit beside a word; the state is in the words as well. The keys only show the look: digits in ink, an operator in the second colour, the one equals key in yellow. Each sinks a little while pressed.

calculator

Mmemory in use,0

The layout is after the Braun ET 66 of 1987 (Dietrich Lubs and Dieter Rams), not an exact copy. It works as that kind of pocket calculator does: each operation runs as you press the next, so 2 + 3 × 4 gives 20; pressing = again repeats the last step; 200 + 10 % = gives 220; MRC recalls the memory and, pressed twice, clears it; dividing by zero shows Error until C. Once you have pressed a key you can type too: digits, . or comma, + − * /, Enter or = for equals, Escape for C, Delete for CE and Backspace to remove a digit. Space presses the focused key.

how to use

Copy gute-form.css and its fonts folder next to your page, and link the CSS. It loads Inter from that folder, so no request leaves your site:

<link rel="stylesheet" href="gute-form.css">
<body class="gf-page">

A panel is a label strip with a heading, then a body:

<section class="gf-panel" aria-labelledby="p1">
  <div class="gf-panel__strip">
    <h2 class="gf-panel__title" id="p1">tuner</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="gf-panel__body">
    <label class="gf-label" for="f1">station</label>
    <input class="gf-field" id="f1">
    <div class="gf-buttons">
      <button class="gf-button" type="button">cancel</button>
      <button class="gf-button gf-button--accent">store</button>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

For a palette of your own, set the tokens on :root after the link. Keep text 4.5:1 and --gf-line 3:1 against --gf-page and --gf-surface:

:root {
  --gf-page: #f4f2ec;      /* page, and the fill of fields */
  --gf-surface: #e7e4dc;   /* panels, buttons, selects */
  --gf-line: #7d786f;      /* 1px seams */
  --gf-ink: #26251f;       /* text, ticks, the focus ring */
  --gf-muted: #5e5a52;     /* printed labels */
  --gf-accent: #2e2f30;    /* the main button */
  --gf-accent-ink: #f4f2ec;
  --gf-second: #8a5a36;    /* "on" states only */
  --gf-on-second: #f4f2ec;
  --gf-trim: #b98d63;      /* the material: side strips */
  --gf-error: #b3261e;     /* errors, always with a symbol and words */
}

The calculator is a .gf-calc: an <output class="gf-calc__display"> over a .gf-keypad of real buttons, each named by a data-key. The CSS only draws it; the arithmetic and the keyboard are in this page’s script.

The turning SK 4 at the top comes from an optional add-on, gute-form-3d.js, which draws it with three.js. Add an import map and the module to your <head>, and mark a .gf-product figure with data-gf-3d="hero". data-gf-3d-turn is spin (a slow full turn), sway or off, and data-gf-3d-trim is wood or aluminium; drag to turn it. Without WebGL, under reduced data or forced colours, or if the download fails, the flat drawing stays:

<script type="importmap">
{ "imports": {
  "three": "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.186.1/build/three.module.js",
  "three/addons/": "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.186.1/examples/jsm/"
} }
</script>
<script type="module" src="gute-form-3d.js"></script>

<figure class="gf-product" data-gf-3d="hero" data-gf-3d-turn="spin">

Write labels in lowercase, as Braun printed them. The comment at the top of gute-form.css gives the markup for every component; this page’s own script (tabs, menus, the dialog, the mixed check box, the calculator) is at the bottom of its source.
